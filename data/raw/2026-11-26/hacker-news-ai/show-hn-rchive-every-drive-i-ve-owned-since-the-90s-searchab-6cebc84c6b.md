RCHIVE is a cross-platform desktop app for cataloging, searching and reorganizing files on your computers and drives. Like, all of 'em: internal, external, network shares, USB sticks, loose SD cards, weird CD-R's, DVDs, floppy disks, Zip disks, and everything in between.
I built this thing because my digital archives are an embarrassment! 30 years of getting a new hard drive whenever capacity quadrupled and dragging the old one to it into a folder titled "BACKUP - TO SORT" will do that to you lol, especially when you know deep down that "sorting" will never happen. To try to deal with that growing mess on a daily basis, I've tried a patchwork of all of our favorites in the past -- Everything, grep, custom scripts, you name it. And don't get me started on built-in OS defaults like Windows Explorer, Finder, Spotlight. I figured it was time to make something better-suited to my tastes, and 4200 commits later here we are!
RCHIVE scans as many different sources as you throw at it, gives you unified deep search across the entire catalog (with drives connected or disconnected), allows you to virtually organize and clean up giant archives of digital stuff by applying recipes and transformations, resiliently copies or moves files when you're ready to actually restructure on disk, or just lets you quickly pop in to find something or browse everything with previews, metadata, etc.
It works on Windows, Mac or Linux, works offline, has zero telemetry, deletes nothing, and is fully deterministic in operation. So, no wacky AI integrations for decision making or classification, everything is predictable and repeatable, you can even run portable versions off of a USB drive if you don't want to install it.
I've been using it all day every day for a year and am just stoked to have it out there, get some users, get some feedback (good or bad), and see what people think. There's a free trial, no email address or anything needed.
The website is also full of videos, features, comparisons, all that stuff, as well as a
ncdu
-like companion app for figuring out where the hell all your disk space went.
Anyways, check it out! Should come in handy if you're ever in Grand Central trying to find a garbage file left on some dude's Gibson.
