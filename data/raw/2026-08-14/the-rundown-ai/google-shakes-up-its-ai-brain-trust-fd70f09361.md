**LATEST DEVELOPMENTS**

Image source: Images 2.0 / The Rundown

**The Rundown:** Google just reshuffled its AI leadership, with DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis leaving his role to be the unit’s chairman and Alphabet’s chief scientist, and 27-year veteran Jeff Dean leaving to co-found a startup focused on scientific discovery.

**The details:** 

- Hassabis, a key figure in AI, hands daily control to CTO Koray Kavukcuoglu, who will steer work on frontier models, including Gemini 4, as senior VP.
- In the new role, Hassabis will focus on “strategic and global AGI matters” while advising DeepMind and leading Isomorphic Labs’ drug-discovery efforts.
- Dean is starting Discovery Loop, a PBC aiming to automate scientific research, joined by Google veterans Sanjay Ghemawat, Oriol Vinyals, and Quoc Le.
- Google’s move comes as pressure mounts over Gemini 3.5 Pro delays and top researchers leaving for rivals that continue to deliver frontier releases.

**Why it matters:** This marks a new phase of AI for Google. Hassabis and Dean shaped the company’s AI efforts for over a decade. Now, as the two architects behind its success step into new chapters, all eyes will be on how Google preserves its research edge, closes its execution gaps, and keeps pace with rivals at the frontier.

**The Rundown:** Each week, Rundown AI educator Nate Grahek breaks down what he’s seeing while teaching real teams to use AI.

**Nate:** Last weekend, I fixed my mom’s garage door keypad with my phone sitting on the workbench. Both hands stayed on the problem the whole time; I just talked. That’s new.

For years, voice mode was a distraction: small brain, no memory, and really no idea who you were. Now OpenAI and Anthropic have shipped dueling live voice upgrades, and the coverage is all about how natural they sound.

I care about something else: voice can finally read your context files. You’d never pay a consultant who pitches a strategy without knowing your business. Same rule here.

My voice checklist: upload as much context as you can, tell it to use a framework you already trust (I like WRAP from the book Decisive), ask it to interview you to fill the gaps, and prompt it to push back on your weak ideas.

That last step is the important one. Don’t settle for an AI that agrees with you. A real thought partner argues back and coaches you through a proven framework. Now it comes along on a walk.

###### META

Image source: Meta Superintelligence Labs

**The Rundown:** Meta just released a beta terminal coding agent called Muse Code, alongside the model powering it, Muse Spark 1.2, marking a direct move onto the turf dominated by OpenAI’s Codex and Anthropic’s Claude Code.

**The details:** 

- Muse Code runs background sub-agents that build session context and work in parallel, with Meta saying it built six game features at once without collisions.
- Muse Spark 1.2, a coding-centric update, hit 54 on Artificial Analysis’s Intelligence Index — a major climb since April, now third among U.S. labs.
- The model’s biggest gains are in agentic knowledge work, its old weak spot, with GDPval score leaping 260 Elo points to rank just behind frontier models.
- API pricing remains the same as 1.1 at $1.25/$4.25 per million tokens, but a new contributor tier runs ~21x cheaper if you let Meta train on your prompts.

**Why it matters:** A year ago, Meta was the lab that had fallen behind. Since April, it’s shipped a new model line, an image generator, and now its own coding agent, all undercutting rivals on price. It’s a major turnaround. The thing that remains to be seen is how Meta will approach open source (as Zuckerberg teased) with these systems.
