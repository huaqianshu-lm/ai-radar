###### NATE’S NOTEBOOK

**The Rundown:** *Each week, Rundown AI educator Nate Grahek breaks down what he's seeing while teaching real teams to use AI.***Nate:** Most of the frustrated people I teach aren't really skeptics. They asked AI for something big, got slop back, and filed the comfortable verdict: see, it's still dumb. The output was bad. You were also aiming it wrong. Both things are true.

Here's my hypothesis for why smart people get stuck: they use AI for work outside their domain. Writing code, drafting contracts — it feels like superpowers. But in your own field, you know what good looks like in three seconds. Outside it, you're just hoping. If you can't judge it at a glance, don't ship it.

Now think about what real work goes through: research, testing, cutting, rearranging. Then, last, the finished thing. Most people hand AI that final verb and skip everything before it. Backwards. The early verbs are where AI does its best work and where you can steer. Go wider and deeper than you ever could, then take the last verb yourself.

One more rule: tell it upfront what format you work in (Word doc, spreadsheet, deck) so the handoff lands where you finish.

###### GAMGEE

Image source: Gamgee

**The Rundown:** Paul Conyngham introduced Gamgee, a Y Combinator-backed startup building mRNA cancer shots matched to the mutations in a single dog's tumor, coming just months after he made one with ChatGPT and AlphaFold for his own dog Rosie.

**The details:** 

- Several vet visits over 11 months missed Rosie's mast cell cancer, and once surgery, chemo, and immunotherapy all failed, her outlook fell to just months.
- Conyngham used AI tools for the science and analysis, with UNSW then developing a personalized vaccine that helped reduce Rosie’s tumors.
- Sam Altman met with Conyngham in March following the initial story, saying it had him thinking that “this should be a company”.
- Australian vets are hosting the first trials, with Gamgee taking cases worldwide and managing the custom design, treatment, and monitoring end-to-end.

**Why it matters:** Saving dogs is a mission we can get behind, and Conyngham’s story is an amazing example of being able to just do things in the AI age. The hope is also that the start of personalized cancer treatments doesn’t end at dogs, and AI is going to be a major driver of pushing the same sort of research forward into the human realm.
