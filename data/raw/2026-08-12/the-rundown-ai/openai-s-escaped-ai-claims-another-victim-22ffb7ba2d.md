###### OPENAI & SECURITY

Image source: Hugging Face

**The Rundown:** OpenAI's rogue agent story just widened, with a new company confirming a customer of its cloud platform as the hack's second victim, and fresh Hugging Face forensics timeline tallying 17,600 hostile actions by the models.

**The details:** 

- AI infrastructure company Modal Labs told Reuters the agent got in through a customer's coding flaw that left a sandbox usable by anyone online.
- OAI's own Tuesday update revealed it found break-ins at four accounts, with the unreleased model now "deactivated, encrypted, and restricted."
- A new HF timeline detailed the expansive effort taken by the models, analyzed via GLM-5.2 after Anthropic’s model guardrails flagged the analysis.
- Sam Altman said more companies "could be" on the hacked list beyond HF and Modal, and that the unreleased system is off for good with training paused.

**Why it matters:** Altman said he's "a little surprised" more people aren't feeling this the way he is about the incident, and it’s clear this breach has changed the vibes — which is likely why Altman spent the week floating a slowdown on podcasts and taking meetings in D.C. We’ll see if the fears actually trickle down into release timelines.

###### OPENAI

Image source: @AlenHe on X

**The Rundown:** CEO Sam Altman spent Wednesday on Capitol Hill meeting with senators about OpenAI's next models and addressing the security incident, hours after President Trump floated AI "controls" when asked about the company's rogue agent.

**The details:** 

- Altman reportedly previewed OAI’s upcoming models, but no new details on capabilities emerged, and the CEO is “not sure” about a release timeline.
- Trump said the U.S. is looking at controls, but has no interest in blocking firms from shipping products and potentially losing the AI lead to China.
- Altman said he "wouldn’t use the word ‘deceleration’, but we do need to talk about the need to pace it” in regards to an AI slowdown.
- The White House owes a voluntary vetting framework for advanced AI models by Aug. 1, with drafts already sent to OAI, Anthropic, and Google for feedback.

**Why it matters:** Just weeks ago, the talk in Washington was about setting Fable and Mythos loose, and now it's about slowing AI down and asking the government for help. Things move fast here, but whatever Altman was actually previewing behind closed doors may end up being the best signal yet of where the situation is heading.
